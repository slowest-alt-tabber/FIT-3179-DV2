AUSTRALIAN HIGHER EDUCATION

Finishing the Degree

Why do some students take longer to graduate?

Completion varies across study pathways and student groups. Experience and location add further context. Explore the patterns behind Australian university outcomes.

From commencement to completion

01 / THE COMPLETION JOURNEY

Completion takes time

Early results capture only part of each student's journey.

Completion across follow-up periods

Part-time study, course transfers and interruptions can delay graduation. Longer follow-up captures students who finish beyond the expected course duration.

Outcomes after nine years

Financial pressure, caring duties and course mismatch may contribute to early departure. Flexible re-entry can help some students return later.

02 / DIFFERENT PATHWAYS

Students do not follow identical paths

Course structures and entry preparation may shape progress.

Nine-year completion by study field

Professional placements and accreditation rules can shape course length. Student backgrounds and course structures also differ across disciplines.

Completion outcomes by university entry rank

Entry rank reflects earlier schooling and opportunity. Bridging courses, teaching support and financial pressures may also influence progress after enrolment.

03 / THE STUDENT EXPERIENCE

Experience has several dimensions

Teaching, resources and connection create different experiences.

Undergraduate and postgraduate ratings

Postgraduates may have clearer study goals or more experience. Work and family commitments can leave less time for campus activities and peer contact.

Provider ratings in 2017 and 2025

Online delivery, teaching changes and student needs may affect experience ratings. Compare providers to see which improved and which declined.

How experience ratings vary across fields

Peer engagement tends to receive weaker ratings. Shared activities need time and access. Students can use learning resources more independently.

Experience ratings by study field

Computing reports stronger resource ratings than overall experience ratings. Feedback, workload and career relevance may shape that gap.

How experience measures change rank

Skills and resources often rank strongly. Peer engagement tends to rank lower. The moving lines show whether this pattern changes.

04 / PLACE AND OPPORTUNITY

Location adds another layer

Student support and completion do not vary identically.

Student support ratings by state

State averages depend on represented providers. New South Wales contributes the largest sample. Smaller samples give individual providers greater influence.

Student support at sampled campuses

Support can depend on staff capacity and service access. Major cities and regional centres face different constraints.

Completion and support across states

Some states combine stronger completion with moderate support ratings. Others show weaker results across both measures.

THE BIG PICTURE

Three takeaways

Time changes the picture

Longer follow-up captures students who graduate later.

Pathways differ

Study field and entry preparation relate to completion.

Experience matters

Support and peer connection show room for improvement.

These patterns identify questions for further investigation. They do not establish direct causes of completion.
