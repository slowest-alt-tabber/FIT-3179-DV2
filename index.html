AUSTRALIAN HIGHER EDUCATION ANALYTICS

Finishing the Degree

Who completes university, how do outcomes shift over time, and what role does student satisfaction play?

Commencing a university degree represents a significant investment of time, ambition, and financial resources. Yet, across Australian higher education, progression rates vary substantially. By examining official Department of Education cohort tracking alongside national Quality Indicators for Learning and Teaching (QILT) survey data, this dashboard explores the multi-faceted landscape of student completion, demographic equity, and campus experiences.

01 — The Big Picture: National Completion Trends

University completion is not a single point in time—it is an extended journey. While approximately 42%–44% of commencing bachelor students graduate within four years, allowing additional follow-up time reveals a far higher long-term attainment rate.

Published Completion Rates Across Cohort Periods

National cohort tracking displays steady long-term stability across published cohorts. While four-year completion rates sit between 42%–44%, extending tracking to six and nine years pushes completion up to 56%–58% and ~62% respectively—a +20 percentage point gain reflecting widespread flexible and part-time study pathways.

Completion Rates by Field of Education

Completion outcomes vary dramatically across academic disciplines. Professional accreditation disciplines such as Health and Education boast completion rates above 70%, whereas broad or technical fields like Information Technology experience higher drop-out rates.

Undergraduate vs. Postgraduate Experience Ratings

Comparing student satisfaction levels reveals significant shifts between study levels. While Student Support and Services and Teaching Quality show substantial positive gains for Postgraduate coursework students, Peer Engagement drops, reflecting more independent and flexible study routines.

National Breakdown of Published Outcomes

At the national level, approximately 62% of commencing domestic bachelor students complete their qualification over a nine-year window, while roughly 20% leave after their first year and never return, leaving 18% who re-enroll or take extended leaves.

02 — Equity & Student Pathways: Who Completes?

Student retention and academic success are deeply intertwined with entry qualifications, educational background, and student characteristics.

Completion Outcomes Across ATAR Bands & Characteristics

Academic preparation upon entry strongly predicts university completion. Students entering with an ATAR above 90 achieve completion rates exceeding 80%, whereas lower ATAR brackets display higher rates of early attrition and re-enrollment delay.

Early Completion vs. Longer-Term Trajectories

Comparing short-term (4-year) and long-term (9-year) outcomes demonstrates that non-traditional and part-time student groups make substantial gains over longer observation horizons, narrowing the initial gap seen in early graduation metrics.

03 — The Student Experience: Satisfaction & Engagement

Understanding completion requires looking beyond numerical completion rates to the underlying student experience. The national Quality Indicators for Learning and Teaching (QILT) survey evaluates teaching quality, peer engagement, and learning resources.

Rating Distributions Across Experience Indicators

Across Australian universities, ratings for Skills Development and Learning Resources consistently surpass 80% positive feedback. In contrast, Peer Engagement displays widespread variance and lower overall ratings.

Student Experience Ratings by Field of Education

Field-level analysis indicates that satisfaction with Teaching Quality and Skills Development remains high across most disciplines, whereas fields such as Computing and Engineering register lower peer engagement satisfaction.

Comparative Experience Profiles Across Universities

Connecting institutional performance metrics reveals that universities scoring high in teaching quality do not always perform equally well in learner support, reflecting distinct institutional profiles and campus delivery models.

04 — Geographic Perspectives: Regional Equity & Disability Outcomes

Geographic equity in Australian higher education extends beyond general satisfaction. Comparing completion outcomes for students with disability across states and territories highlights regional disparities in support and retention.

Statewide Mean Student Support Ratings

Aggregating institutional support scores by state highlights regional consistency across major states (NSW, VIC, QLD), while smaller territories exhibit distinct average satisfaction profiles reflecting smaller student populations.

Institution Locations and Student Ratings

Mapping individual main campuses illustrates the geographic concentration of Australian higher education along the coastal capitals, displaying specific positive rating scores for each university.

Disability Student Completion Rates by State

Utilizing a cartogram tile grid to eliminate land-area distortions, this map reveals completion outcomes for students with a disability. Victoria (63.5%) and ACT (60.0%) lead national benchmarks, while several regions hover below 50%.

About the Data & Methodology

Completion Rates: Derived from the Australian Government Department of Education 2024 Section 17 Completion Rates dataset. Cohort tracking follows commencing domestic undergraduate bachelor cohorts across specified 4-year, 6-year, and 9-year follow-up windows.

Student Experience: Sourced from the Quality Indicators for Learning and Teaching (QILT) Student Experience Survey (SES). Survey metrics report positive rating percentages (%) across key focus areas.

Analytical Note: The relationship between institutional completion rates and QILT survey indicators represents an association across cohorts rather than direct individual causal tracking.
