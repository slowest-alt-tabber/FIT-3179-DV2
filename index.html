University Completion Rates

Who completes a degree? How do outcomes vary?

Explore completion by study field, entry qualifications, and location. Compare outcomes with student experience ratings.

Four perspectives. One national story.

01 / COMPLETION TIMELINES

When do students complete?

Study pathways and interruptions can change completion timelines.

Completion across follow-up periods

Part-time study, course transfers and temporary breaks may delay graduation. Longer pathways can extend beyond the standard course duration.

Outcomes after nine years

Financial pressure, workload and course fit may prompt students to leave. Flexible re-entry options may help some return when circumstances improve.

02 / STUDENT PATHWAYS

Who completes a degree?

Course structure and prior preparation may shape student progress.

Completion by field of education

Accreditation, clinical placements and prescribed course sequences can shape study pace. Entry requirements and student backgrounds also differ across disciplines.

Completion outcomes by ATAR band

ATAR can reflect preparation for university-level study. School opportunities, pathway entry and financial pressures may also shape how students progress.

03 / STUDENT EXPERIENCE

What is university life like?

Study format, resources and student needs can shape experience.

Undergraduate versus postgraduate ratings

Postgraduates may navigate services with greater confidence. Work and family commitments can leave less time for campus activities and peer contact.

Overall experience: 2017 versus 2025

Ratings may shift with student demographics, online delivery and teaching changes. Support investment and campus services may also shape how students judge their experience.

Ratings across experience indicators

Learning resources can be used independently. Peer engagement depends on shared time and interaction, which work and commuting may limit.

Experience ratings by study field

Computing students may value reliable software and digital resources. Teaching quality, workload, feedback and career relevance also shape overall experience.

Experience profiles across study fields

Practical subjects may depend heavily on specialist resources. Large cohorts or limited shared activities may make meaningful peer connections harder to build.

04 / REGIONAL PERSPECTIVES

Where do regional differences appear?

Provider mix, campus access and local support may shape outcomes.

Mean student support ratings by state

State averages depend on which providers are represented. Smaller provider samples give each institution more influence over the reported result.

Campus locations and student support ratings

Campus support may depend on adviser access, staff capacity and service availability. Large cities and regional centres face different access and staffing constraints.

Disability completion by state

Timely adjustments, accessible course design and support may influence student progress. Course mix and local service availability may also contribute.

DATA / METHODS

About the data

Completion rates

Department of Education cohort data cover four-, six-, and nine-year follow-up periods.

Student experience

QILT survey data report positive ratings for teaching, support, resources, skills, and peer engagement.

Disability completion

The tile map uses the state values in the supplied disability-completion CSV.

Interpreting the findings

Suggested explanations are plausible contributing factors. The datasets do not directly test these causes.
